# CareerBridge
## Project Documentation

**Project name:** CareerBridge  
**Project area:** Career and education guidance  
**First focus:** Students in Nagaland  
**Main student groups:** Class 10 and Class 12 students  
**Long-term focus:** The states of Northeast India

---

## 1. Project introduction

CareerBridge is a platform that helps students understand their education and career choices.

Students often reach Class 10 or Class 12 without knowing what they want to do next. Some students do not know which subjects or careers may suit them. Others know a few popular options but do not know about the many other paths available. Information about courses, colleges, entrance examinations, and scholarships is also spread across different places.

CareerBridge brings this information into one guided platform. It asks students simple questions about themselves, shows them possible directions, explains those directions, and helps them decide what to explore next.

CareerBridge is not meant to choose a career for a student. It is meant to help students make better decisions for themselves.

> **CareerBridge guides students. It does not decide their future.**

---

## 2. Why this project is needed

Choosing what to do after Class 10 or Class 12 can be difficult. Students may face the following problems:

- They may not know what career choices are available.
- They may know the name of a course but not know what kind of work it can lead to.
- They may not understand the difference between a course, a career, a pathway, and a college.
- They may not know whether a choice is available after Class 10 or after Class 12.
- They may not know which entrance examinations are connected to a course or career.
- They may not know which scholarships they can apply for.
- They may find it difficult to compare different choices.
- They may receive advice that is too general or not suitable for their situation.
- They may have limited access to a career counsellor.
- They may find information online, but the information may be incomplete or outdated.

These problems can make students feel confused, pressured, or afraid of making the wrong choice.

CareerBridge is designed to make the process easier to understand. It gives students a clear place to begin and helps them move one step at a time.

---

## 3. Aim of the project

The aim of CareerBridge is to help Class 10 and Class 12 students understand their education and career possibilities and take a suitable next step with more confidence.

The project begins with Nagaland because the first set of information is focused on the state. The project is designed so that information from other Northeastern states can be added later.

CareerBridge is intended to be useful to real students. It should not be treated only as a demonstration project. The information should be understandable, responsible, and connected to reliable sources.

---

## 4. Objectives of the project

### 4.1 Help students understand themselves

CareerBridge asks students about the subjects they enjoy, their interests, strengths, goals, values, preferences, and practical needs.

The purpose is not to give students a permanent label. The purpose is to help them notice the types of work and study that may be worth exploring.

### 4.2 Show different possibilities

The platform should show more than the most popular careers. Students should be able to discover academic, diploma, vocational, professional, technical, creative, service, business, and other routes.

The platform may highlight a few good starting points so that the student is not overwhelmed. However, the student should still be able to see and explore other possibilities.

### 4.3 Explain each suggestion

When a direction is shown to a student, the platform should explain why it appeared. The explanation may be connected to the student’s interests, subjects, strengths, goals, values, location, budget, or work preferences.

The student should not be expected to trust an unexplained result.

### 4.4 Connect career choices with education choices

CareerBridge should help students understand how a broad career direction connects to:

- Careers and job roles.
- Study routes.
- Courses.
- Colleges and other institutions.
- Entrance examinations.
- Scholarships.
- Skills to build.
- Practical next steps.

### 4.5 Help students compare choices

Students should be able to compare two options instead of trying to remember information from different pages. They may compare two courses, careers, pathways, or institutions.

The purpose of comparison is to show differences. It is not to announce that one choice is always better than another.

### 4.6 Help students make a plan

After exploring a direction, students should be able to create a simple action plan. The plan can include checking an official website, learning about eligibility, finding a suitable course, reviewing institutions, checking scholarships, or discussing the choice with a trusted person.

### 4.7 Provide regional information

The first version of CareerBridge focuses on Nagaland. The project should later support Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Sikkim, and Tripura.

## 5. Target users

### 5.1 Main users

CareerBridge is mainly for:

- Students studying in Class 10.
- Students who have completed Class 10.
- Students studying in Class 12.
- Students who have completed Class 12.
- Students waiting for their results.
- Students who are unsure about their next step.

### 5.2 Other people who may use the platform

The platform may also be useful for:

- Parents and guardians.
- Teachers.
- Career counsellors.
- Mentors.
- School and college support staff.
- Organisations that help students with education and employment.

These users may use the platform to help a student understand and discuss different choices.

---

## 6. Geographic focus

### 6.1 Nagaland as the starting point

CareerBridge begins with Nagaland because students need information that is connected to their own region. A student may want to know which institutions are nearby, which options are available within the state, which routes require moving to another state, and which scholarships may apply to them.

A general career website may not answer these regional questions clearly. CareerBridge is intended to make regional information easier to find.

### 6.2 Future expansion to Northeast India

The project is planned for the wider Northeast region. The same main process can be used for each state:

1. Collect reliable state and regional information.
2. Review and organize the information.
3. Connect it to the correct courses, careers, pathways, and student stages.
4. Show it only to students for whom it is relevant.

National and Northeast-wide opportunities should remain available to students when they meet the requirements.

---

## 7. Main idea of the platform

CareerBridge is organized around a simple student journey:

```text
Understand yourself
        ↓
Explore possible directions
        ↓
Learn about careers and study routes
        ↓
Compare choices
        ↓
Plan the next step
```

The platform should always make the following clear:

- Where the student is now.
- What the current page is about.
- What the student can do next.
- Whether the information is general guidance or an official current fact.

---

## 8. Student journey in detail

### Step 1: Start

The student enters CareerBridge and learns what the platform does. The student chooses whether they are mainly at the Class 10 or Class 12 stage.

The platform should explain that there are no “correct” answers in the counselling questions. The student should feel comfortable saying that they are unsure.

### Step 2: Answer simple questions

The student answers questions about their interests, subjects, strengths, goals, preferences, and situation.

The questions should be written in simple language. They should not assume that the student already knows career terms.

The student should be able to:

- Move through the questions one at a time.
- See their progress.
- Go back and change an answer.
- Choose “Not sure yet” when necessary.
- Continue later if they do not finish in one session.

### Step 3: Review the profile

The platform shows the student what it understood from their answers. The student can review and edit the information.

This page should not make the student feel that the answers have decided everything. It should explain that the profile is only a starting point for exploration.

### Step 4: See possible directions

The platform shows several fields or directions that may be worth exploring. The most useful starting points can be shown first, while other possibilities remain available.

For each direction, the platform should show:

- What the direction is about.
- Why it may fit some of the student’s answers.
- What the student can learn next.
- A clear button to open more information.

### Step 5: Understand a direction

The student opens one direction and learns:

- What people in that field do.
- What subjects may help.
- What skills may be useful.
- What kinds of careers exist in the field.
- What study routes are available.
- What challenges the student should think about.
- What other directions may also be worth exploring.

The page should give enough information to help the student understand the direction without forcing an immediate decision.

### Step 6: Explore study routes

The student learns how people commonly enter the field. This may include:

- Choosing a stream after Class 10.
- Choosing a higher-secondary route.
- Joining a diploma or vocational course.
- Studying an undergraduate course.
- Taking an entrance examination.
- Building skills through another route.

The platform should explain that more than one route may lead toward a similar career.

### Step 7: Compare options

The student can compare two courses, careers, pathways, or institutions. The comparison may include duration, eligibility, subjects, entrance requirements, location, cost information, further study, and possible outcomes.

The page should help the student think about which differences matter to them.

### Step 8: Make a plan

The student creates a list of practical tasks. The plan may include checking official information, talking to a teacher, reviewing a course, visiting an institution website, checking a scholarship, or preparing for an examination.

The plan should help the student move forward without making them feel rushed.

### Step 9: Ask for help

The student can use the Mentor to ask questions about the information they are exploring. The Mentor should support understanding and comparison. It should not replace an official notice, a teacher, a counsellor, or a family discussion.

## 9. Types of information in CareerBridge

CareerBridge uses different types of information. Each type has a different purpose.

### 9.1 Career fields

A career field is a broad area such as technology, healthcare, education, business, agriculture, media, design, public service, or engineering.

A field may contain many different careers. It should help students understand a direction before they focus on one job title.

### 9.2 Careers

A career is a type of work. A career description should explain what people do, what skills they use, where they may work, how they may enter the field, and what challenges may exist.

### 9.3 Pathways

A pathway is a route from the student’s present stage toward further study or work. It may be academic, diploma-based, vocational, professional, or another recognized route.

### 9.4 Courses

A course is a particular education or training programme. Course information may include the level, duration, subjects, eligibility, entrance requirements, possible career directions, further study, and fee information.

### 9.5 Institutions

An institution is a school, college, university, training centre, polytechnic, ITI, or other education provider.

### 9.6 Examinations

An examination may be needed for admission, recruitment, professional entry, or another education-related purpose. The information should explain who conducts it, who may apply, and where the student should confirm current dates and requirements.

### 9.7 Scholarships

A scholarship may help students pay for their education. Information should include the provider, eligibility, amount description, required documents, applicable stage, deadline information, and official source.

## 10. Recommendation approach

CareerBridge uses the student’s answers to organize possible directions. It should not present the result as a prediction.

### 10.1 What the platform may consider

The platform may consider:

- Subjects the student enjoys.
- Subjects the student finds difficult.
- Interests.
- Strengths.
- Goals.
- Values.
- Preferred work style.
- Location preference.
- Budget concerns.
- Scholarship needs.
- Institution preferences.
- Hostel needs.
- Other information shared by the student.

### 10.2 What the platform should not claim

The platform should not claim:

- That a student is guaranteed to succeed in a career.
- That a student is unsuitable for a career based on a few answers.
- That a percentage is the student’s chance of success.
- That one option is the only correct choice.
- That the platform knows the student better than the student knows themselves.

### 10.3 How recommendations should be explained

A recommendation should use simple explanations such as:

- “You mentioned that you enjoy Mathematics.”
- “You showed interest in working with people.”
- “This direction includes routes that can begin after Class 10.”
- “This option may be worth comparing if staying near home is important to you.”

The explanation should help the student think. It should not pressure them to accept the suggestion.

## 11. Data plan

### 11.1 Workbook data for catalogue information

Structured and changing catalogue information can be maintained through Excel workbooks. The workbooks may contain information about institutions, examinations, scholarships, courses, or other education-related records.

The information should not be placed directly in front of students as soon as a workbook is uploaded. It should go through a review process.

### 11.2 Review process for workbook information

The intended process is:

```text
Prepare workbook
      ↓
Upload workbook
      ↓
Read and organize rows
      ↓
Find missing or unclear information
      ↓
Review the rows
      ↓
Approve suitable information
      ↓
Show approved information to students
```

The original workbook information should be preserved so that changes can be checked later.

### 11.3 Information from official sources

Changing or sensitive information should be connected to an official source. This includes:

- Admission dates.
- Examination dates.
- Application deadlines.
- Eligibility requirements.
- Fees.
- Course availability.
- Scholarship deadlines.
- Current institution details.

The platform should save the official URL and the date on which the information was checked.

### 11.4 Why the platform should not depend on random web scraping

Automatically collecting information from any website may produce information that is incomplete, outdated, or difficult to verify. It may also break when a website changes its design.

CareerBridge should prefer official pages, official notices, and controlled updates. If a source cannot be confirmed, the platform should clearly say that the information needs verification.

### 11.5 Meaning of missing information

Missing information should not be treated as a negative fact.

For example:

- A blank hostel field does not mean that hostel facilities do not exist.
- A missing website does not mean that an institution has no website.
- A missing fee does not mean that the course is free.
- A missing deadline does not mean that there is no deadline.

The platform should use words such as “not available,” “not provided,” or “needs verification.”

## 12. Nagaland-first catalogue

The first collection of regional information is based on Nagaland.

The documented catalogue includes approximately:

- 423 institutions.
- 79 entrance examinations.
- 66 scholarships.

The institution information includes schools, colleges, institutes, training centres, polytechnics, ITIs, universities, and medical or nursing providers.

The examination information includes national, state, institution-level, professional, recruitment, olympiad, and other relevant examinations.

The scholarship information includes central, national, Northeast-wide, private, corporate, and state schemes.

These numbers describe the supplied catalogue material. They should be checked again after the information is loaded into the working database.

### 12.1 Limits of the first catalogue

The first catalogue may not contain complete information for every institution or scheme. Some records may not include:

- An official website.
- A phone number or email address.
- Hostel information.
- Exact location coordinates.
- A complete structured list of courses.
- Current admission dates.

These gaps should be shown honestly and improved over time.

## 13. Responsible use of information

CareerBridge should help students investigate their choices responsibly.

### 13.1 General guidance

General descriptions can help a student understand what a field or career is like. This information may remain useful for a long time, although it should still be reviewed periodically.

### 13.2 Current official information

Current facts can change. A student should be directed to the official source before relying on information about:

- Dates.
- Fees.
- Eligibility.
- Seats.
- Application rules.
- Scholarship deadlines.
- Course availability.

### 13.3 Student responsibility

CareerBridge can organize information and suggest questions to consider. The student, family, teacher, or counsellor should confirm important decisions using official information.

### 13.4 Mentor responsibility

The Mentor should say when it does not have enough reliable information. It should not fill gaps by guessing.

---

## 14. Privacy and safety

Many CareerBridge users may be minors. The platform should collect only information that is needed for guidance.

The project should avoid requiring:

- Exact home addresses.
- Private financial documents.
- Unnecessary personal information.
- Sensitive information unrelated to the student’s choices.

Students should be able to begin exploring without being forced to create an account immediately. An account may become useful when the student wants to save progress, saved items, comparisons, or action plans.

Student information should be protected so that one student cannot see another student’s profile or saved information.

Administrative tools should be available only to authorized people who are responsible for reviewing and maintaining catalogue information.

## 15. Expected benefits

### 15.1 Benefits for students

Students may benefit from:

- A clearer understanding of their own interests and strengths.
- More awareness of career and study choices.
- Easier comparison of different routes.
- Better access to regional institutions and opportunities.
- Greater awareness of examinations and scholarships.
- A practical list of next steps.
- Less dependence on incomplete or random information.

### 15.2 Benefits for parents and teachers

Parents and teachers may use the platform as a starting point for conversations with students. It can help them discuss several choices instead of focusing only on a small number of popular careers.

### 15.3 Benefits for the region

A reliable regional information platform can help make education opportunities more visible to students who may not have easy access to personal counselling or complete information.

---

## 16. Project boundaries

CareerBridge is a guidance platform. It is not:

- A replacement for a school teacher.
- A replacement for a qualified career counsellor.
- An admission authority.
- A scholarship approval authority.
- An examination conducting body.
- A guarantee of employment.
- A system that can predict a student’s future.

The platform should support decisions while making the limits of its information clear.

---

## 17. Future development direction

The project can develop in the following order:

### 17.1 Improve the Nagaland information

- Review the initial institution data.
- Improve course information.
- Add missing official links.
- Improve district and location information.
- Add freshness and verification details.
- Check examination and scholarship information regularly.

### 17.2 Test the student experience

- Ask students to complete the counselling flow.
- Observe where they become confused.
- Check whether they understand the recommendations.
- Check whether they know what to do next.
- Improve language, layout, and mobile use.

### 17.3 Improve comparison and planning

- Add clearer comparison categories.
- Help students save important differences.
- Make action plans more personal and practical.
- Allow students to revisit an earlier decision without losing their progress.

### 17.4 Add other Northeastern states

After the Nagaland process is stable, information from the other states can be added one state at a time. Each state should have reviewed institutions, scholarships, examinations, local routes, and reliable sources.

### 17.5 Build partnerships

The project may later work with schools, teachers, counsellors, institutions, government departments, and student-support organizations to improve the quality and usefulness of the information.

---

## 18. Success criteria

CareerBridge will be successful when a student can:

1. Begin without feeling confused.
2. Answer the questions in simple language.
3. Understand what the platform learned from them.
4. See several possible directions.
5. Understand why a direction was shown.
6. Find information about related careers and study routes.
7. Compare choices without feeling forced.
8. Check official information before making an important decision.
9. Create one realistic next step.
10. Return later and continue their exploration.

The most important measure of success is not the number of pages or features. It is whether students understand their choices better and feel more capable of taking the next step.

---

## 19. Conclusion

CareerBridge is designed to help students move from confusion to informed exploration.

It begins with the student’s own interests, subjects, strengths, goals, and circumstances. It then connects those answers to possible fields, careers, pathways, courses, institutions, examinations, scholarships, and practical next steps.

The project starts with Nagaland because regional information matters. It is designed to grow across Northeast India because students in the region should have access to guidance that reflects their own educational and geographic situation.

CareerBridge should remain honest about what it knows, clear about what needs verification, and careful not to decide for the student.

Its purpose can be summarized in one sentence:

> **CareerBridge helps students understand their possibilities so they can choose their next step with greater confidence.**
