# CareerBridge
## Project Documentation

**Project type:** Student career and higher-education guidance platform  
**Initial focus:** Class 10 and Class 12 students in Nagaland  
**Long-term focus:** Students across Northeast India  
**Core principle:** **Guide, do not decide.**  
**Document purpose:** Explain the project clearly to students, educators, evaluators, collaborators, administrators, and future maintainers.

---

## 1. Introduction

CareerBridge is a digital career and education guidance platform designed to help students understand what they can do after Class 10 or Class 12. It is initially focused on Nagaland and is designed to expand to the other states of Northeast India.

Many students reach an important academic stage without having a clear understanding of the available career fields, education routes, courses, examinations, institutions, scholarships, or practical next steps. Information may be scattered across different websites, difficult to compare, outdated, or written for people who already understand the education system.

CareerBridge brings these parts of the decision process into one guided experience. It begins by learning about the student’s interests, subjects, strengths, goals, preferences, and practical circumstances. It then presents several possible directions, explains why they may be worth exploring, provides related career and education information, and helps the student compare options and create a practical action plan.

CareerBridge does not claim to know which career a student must choose. It does not treat a student’s answers as a permanent identity. Instead, it provides organized information and transparent guidance so that the student can make a better-informed decision.

> **CareerBridge helps students understand possibilities, compare choices, and plan their next step. It does not decide their future for them.**

---

## 2. Problem statement

Students often face several related problems when deciding what to do after Class 10 or Class 12:

1. They may not know which career fields exist beyond the most commonly discussed options.
2. They may confuse a career, a course, a pathway, and an institution.
3. They may know the name of a course but not understand the work it can lead to.
4. They may not know whether a route is available after Class 10 or only after Class 12.
5. They may find it difficult to compare a degree, diploma, vocational, or professional route.
6. They may not know which entrance examinations or scholarships are relevant.
7. Information about fees, deadlines, eligibility, and admissions may be scattered or outdated.
8. Students from smaller towns and rural areas may have limited access to personal counselling.
9. A student may receive advice that is too general, too confident, or based on incomplete information.

CareerBridge addresses these problems by combining guided self-reflection, structured catalogue information, transparent recommendations, official-source references, comparison tools, and step-by-step planning.

---

## 3. Aim of the project

The aim of CareerBridge is to provide an accessible, trustworthy, and student-friendly platform that helps Class 10 and Class 12 students in Nagaland understand and plan their education and career possibilities.

The project is intended to become useful in real life. It is not designed only as a demonstration of software features. Its design therefore gives importance to data accuracy, clear explanations, responsible recommendations, privacy, maintainability, and the ability to expand to other Northeastern states.

---

## 4. Objectives

The project has the following objectives:

### 4.1 Help students understand themselves

The platform collects structured information about the student’s interests, preferred subjects, strengths, goals, values, work preferences, and practical constraints. The purpose is not to label the student. The purpose is to give the student a useful starting point for exploration.

### 4.2 Present multiple possibilities

CareerBridge should show several possible fields and routes instead of presenting one fixed answer. The system may prioritize a few options so that the student is not overwhelmed, but all appropriate catalogue possibilities should remain accessible.

### 4.3 Explain recommendations

Students should be able to see why a field or pathway was suggested. Explanations may refer to interests, subjects, strengths, goals, values, location, budget, work style, or other information provided by the student.

### 4.4 Connect careers to education routes

The platform should connect broad fields to possible careers, pathways, courses, institutions, examinations, and scholarships. A student should be able to move from a general interest to concrete information about how to investigate that direction.

### 4.5 Support comparison and planning

Students should be able to compare two options and convert a direction into manageable next steps. The system should help students investigate rather than pressure them into an immediate decision.

### 4.6 Improve access to regional information

The first catalogue focuses on Nagaland. The underlying structure is intended to support the wider Northeast so that more regionally relevant information can be added without rebuilding the platform.

### 4.7 Use responsible and verifiable information

Time-sensitive facts should be connected to official or carefully reviewed sources. The platform should communicate uncertainty where information is missing, incomplete, or due for verification.

## 5. Target users

### 5.1 Primary users

The primary users are:

- Students currently studying in Class 10.
- Students who have completed Class 10 and are choosing their next route.
- Students currently studying in Class 12.
- Students who have completed Class 12 or are awaiting results.
- Students who are unsure whether to choose an academic, diploma, vocational, or professional route.

### 5.2 Secondary users

The platform may also help:

- Parents and guardians who want to understand available routes.
- Teachers who want to direct students toward reliable information.
- Career counsellors and mentors who need organized regional information.
- Administrators responsible for maintaining the catalogue.
- Project evaluators and future developers.

### 5.3 User needs

A student should be able to use CareerBridge to answer questions such as:

- What are my possible directions?
- What does this field involve in real life?
- What careers are related to this field?
- What can I study after Class 10 or Class 12?
- Which route is academic, vocational, diploma-based, or professional?
- What entrance examination may be involved?
- Which institutions may be relevant?
- Which scholarships may apply?
- What should I verify from an official source?
- What is the next small step I can take?

## 6. Geographic scope

### 6.1 Initial scope: Nagaland

The first version of the regional catalogue focuses on Nagaland. This allows the project to begin with a manageable and meaningful geographic area rather than attempting to cover the entire country without sufficient local detail.

The initial catalogue includes information about institutions, examinations, scholarships, education routes, and broad career fields relevant to students in the state.

### 6.2 Long-term scope: Northeast India

The long-term scope includes:

- Assam
- Arunachal Pradesh
- Manipur
- Meghalaya
- Mizoram
- Nagaland
- Sikkim
- Tripura

The data model is designed to distinguish state-level, district-level, national, Northeast-wide, and institution-specific information. This is important because a national examination, a Northeast scholarship, and a Nagaland-only scheme should not be treated as the same type of information.

### 6.3 Expansion principle

Expansion should happen only after the Nagaland data workflow is reliable. Adding more states before establishing data review, source tracking, freshness checks, and geographic filtering would increase the amount of information without necessarily improving its trustworthiness.

## 7. Product principles

### 7.1 Guide, do not decide

CareerBridge must not claim that it can predict a student’s future. Recommendations are possibilities worth investigating. They are not guaranteed outcomes, official counselling decisions, or fixed assignments.

### 7.2 Explain before persuading

The platform should explain what a field or route involves before encouraging a student to select it. Students should understand the work, subjects, routes, challenges, and alternatives.

### 7.3 Prioritize without restricting

Showing the top three, five, or ten possibilities can reduce confusion. However, lower-ranked or less obvious options should remain available when they are relevant to the catalogue and the student’s stage.

### 7.4 Make uncertainty visible

If information is unavailable, the interface should say that it is unavailable or needs verification. A blank value must not be presented as proof that a course, hostel, website, or facility does not exist.

### 7.5 Keep the student in control

The student should be able to edit answers, revisit earlier steps, save items, compare alternatives, and change direction.

### 7.6 Separate guidance from official facts

A general explanation of a field can be useful even when no current admission notice exists. However, current fees, deadlines, eligibility, and availability should be connected to official sources and verification information.

## 8. Main student journey

The complete student journey is designed as follows:

```text
1. Learn about CareerBridge
             ↓
2. Select Class 10 or Class 12 stage
             ↓
3. Answer guided counselling questions
             ↓
4. Review and edit the student profile
             ↓
5. Explore prioritized recommendations
             ↓
6. Understand a field and related careers
             ↓
7. Review pathways, courses, and institutions
             ↓
8. Compare two possible options
             ↓
9. Create a practical action plan
             ↓
10. Revisit the journey or ask the Mentor for help
```

The current interface represents the journey through four visible stages:

```text
Your profile → Explore paths → Compare options → Plan next steps
```

Every major page should make the current stage and the next useful action clear.

## 9. Functional modules

### 9.1 Landing and introduction

The landing experience introduces CareerBridge and explains its purpose. It should make counselling the main starting point while still allowing students to browse information directly.

The introduction communicates that the platform helps students understand themselves, explore possibilities, and plan their next step.

### 9.2 Stage selection

The student selects whether they are at the Class 10 or Class 12 stage. Additional stage details may describe whether the student is currently studying, has completed the class, or is awaiting results.

This selection affects the counselling questions and the types of pathways that are emphasized.

### 9.3 Guided counselling

The counselling module collects structured answers through a progressive conversation. The student can move through the questions without needing to understand technical career terminology.

The questions can cover:

- Subjects the student enjoys.
- Subjects the student finds difficult.
- Academic confidence or performance.
- Interests.
- Strengths.
- Goals.
- Values.
- Preferred work style.
- Location preference.
- Budget considerations.
- Scholarship needs.
- Institution preferences.
- Hostel requirements.
- Additional notes.

The counselling flow supports progress indicators, answer validation, previous answers, saved state, and a “Not sure yet” response.

### 9.4 Profile review

After counselling, the student reaches a profile checkpoint. This page confirms what has been understood and allows the student to review or edit answers.

The profile page should not compete with the recommendations page. Its primary action is to continue to recommendations when the profile is sufficiently complete.

### 9.5 Recommendations

The recommendations page presents several broad fields or directions. It prioritizes starting points while preserving access to all available possibilities.

Each recommendation should provide:

- Field name.
- Short explanation.
- Why it appeared.
- Relevant student signals.
- A clear route to see more details.
- A save option where appropriate.

The recommendation page should not make every card expandable when those expansions cause unrelated cards to stretch or leave blank areas. Detailed reasoning belongs on the field detail page or in an independent detail view.

### 9.6 Field exploration

A field is a broad direction rather than one job title. For example, Technology and Computing may contain software, data, support, security, design, and technology-adjacent roles.

A field page should answer four questions:

1. What is this field?
2. What kinds of work exist within it?
3. What routes can lead into it?
4. What should the student think about before choosing it?

### 9.7 Career exploration

A career page describes a role in accessible language. It should explain:

- What people in the role do.
- Which subjects or skills may help.
- Common entry routes.
- Work contexts.
- Possible progression.
- Challenges.
- Alternative routes.
- Questions the student should consider.

### 9.8 Pathway exploration

A pathway describes a route from the student’s current stage toward further study or work. Examples may include a higher-secondary stream, diploma, vocational route, undergraduate route, or professional course.

The pathway page should make clear:

- When the route starts.
- Who it is generally intended for.
- The usual sequence of steps.
- Typical duration where known.
- Related courses and examinations.
- Important notes and limitations.

### 9.9 Course and institution discovery

The course catalogue provides course-level information such as level, duration, eligibility, subjects, entrance requirements, career directions, further study, fees, scholarships, and sources.

The institution catalogue supports search and filtering by information such as:

- District.
- Institution type.
- Ownership.
- Study level.
- Field or course.
- Hostel information.
- Verification state.

Unknown information must be shown as unknown or unavailable. It must not be converted into a negative statement.

### 9.10 Examinations

The examination catalogue may include entrance, recruitment, professional, olympiad, and other relevant examinations.

Information can include:

- Examination name.
- Conducting body.
- Eligibility.
- Applicable stage.
- Preparation information.
- Category.
- Scope.
- State relevance.
- Examination mode.
- Active status.
- Official website.
- Source and verification status.

Current dates and application windows must be confirmed from the official examination website.

### 9.11 Scholarships

The scholarship catalogue may include central, national, Northeast-wide, state-specific, and private or corporate scholarship schemes.

Information can include:

- Provider.
- Eligibility.
- Amount description.
- Deadline description.
- Required documents.
- Applicable student stage.
- Geographic scope.
- Official URL.
- Source URL.
- Verification status.

Geographic applicability should eventually be represented as structured data rather than inferred only from descriptive text.

### 9.12 Comparison

The comparison module lets a student select two options of the same general type and view them side by side.

Possible comparison types include:

- Courses.
- Careers.
- Institutions.
- Pathways.

The comparison should make differences visible in areas such as entry stage, duration, eligibility, subjects, entrance requirements, further study, location, ownership, fees, hostel availability, and verification.

The comparison does not decide which option is better for every student. It supports a student-specific decision.

### 9.13 Saved items

Students can save fields or other catalogue items to revisit later. Saving is useful because career decisions are rarely completed in one session.

### 9.14 Action plans

The action-plan module converts an area of interest into tasks. Tasks may include reviewing eligibility, checking an official notice, comparing pathways, identifying institutions, checking scholarships, or discussing a choice with a trusted person.

The plan is designed to support steady progress, not to pressure the student with an artificial deadline.

### 9.15 AI Mentor

The optional Mentor helps students ask follow-up questions and understand information already present in the trusted catalogue or knowledge sources.

The Mentor should:

- Explain unfamiliar terms.
- Help compare options.
- Point students toward relevant catalogue information.
- Cite or link to source material when factual information is used.
- State when it does not have enough verified information.

The Mentor must not invent facts or present unsupported certainty.

### 9.16 Administration and catalogue maintenance

Administrators can upload workbook data, inspect normalized rows, review warnings and errors, approve acceptable records, reject unsuitable rows, and maintain an audit trail.

The administration workflow is separate from the student interface so that unreviewed information does not silently become public catalogue data.

## 10. Data management model

### 10.1 Dynamic catalogue data

Catalogue information that changes over time should be maintained through a controlled data process. The current design uses workbooks as a practical source for structured catalogue updates.

The intended process is:

```text
Workbook preparation
       ↓
Upload and preserve original file
       ↓
Parse and normalize rows
       ↓
Report warnings, errors, and duplicates
       ↓
Administrator review
       ↓
Approve accepted rows
       ↓
Update canonical catalogue
       ↓
Display to students with source and verification information
```

### 10.2 Sensitive and time-dependent information

Information that can affect a student’s application decision must be handled carefully. This includes:

- Admission deadlines.
- Examination dates.
- Fees.
- Eligibility rules.
- Application windows.
- Seat availability.
- Scholarship deadlines.
- Course availability.
- Current contact details.

These facts should be obtained from official sources or carefully reviewed source material. The platform should store the URL, verification status, and date checked.

### 10.3 Source URL versus current validity

A source URL proves where information came from. It does not prove that the information is still current. A student should be encouraged to open the official source before submitting an application or making a financial decision.

### 10.4 Data states

The catalogue should distinguish between:

- Verified information.
- Information supported by multiple sources.
- Information requiring verification.
- Information that is unavailable.
- Information that may be outdated.

These states should be visible enough to support responsible student decisions without overwhelming the page.

## 11. Initial Nagaland data catalogue

The documented initial catalogue contains three important workbook groups:

| Dataset | Approximate documented records | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Institutions | 423 | Schools, colleges, institutes, training centres, polytechnics, ITIs, universities, and medical or nursing providers |
| Entrance examinations | 79 | National, state, institution-level, professional, recruitment, olympiad, and related examinations |
| Scholarships | 66 | Central, national private or CSR, Northeast-wide, and state scholarship schemes |

The recorded validation found no duplicate stable keys and no missing required values in the primary sheets. The local database should still be checked after import because workbook validation and database verification are separate activities.

### 11.1 Institution limitations

The institution data is useful for discovery but does not provide complete information for every provider.

Some records do not have:

- An official website.
- Contact information.
- Hostel information.
- Exact coordinates.
- A fully structured list of courses.

A blank field must mean “not supplied or not verified,” not “the institution does not provide this.”

### 11.2 District normalization

The source workbooks contain spelling variations in district names. These variations should be mapped to a canonical district list while preserving the original source value for traceability.

### 11.3 Course mapping

The institution workbook contains free-text course descriptions. These should eventually be mapped to controlled course records. Ambiguous descriptions should be reviewed rather than automatically converted into precise claims.

### 11.4 Examination limitations

Examination dates, deadlines, frequency, and eligibility may change. The catalogue should retain official links and verification dates and should not present old workbook text as permanent current information.

### 11.5 Scholarship geography

Scholarships may apply to Nagaland, the whole Northeast, all of India, or another state. Geographic scope should be modeled explicitly so that a student does not receive a misleading list of schemes intended for another state.

## 12. Information architecture

CareerBridge separates five concepts that students often confuse:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Field | A broad area of work or study |
| Career | A specific type of work within or related to a field |
| Pathway | A route that a student can follow toward education or work |
| Course | A particular educational programme |
| Institution | A provider of education or training |

The intended relationship is:

```text
Field
  ↓
Careers
  ↓
Pathways
  ↓
Courses
  ↓
Institutions
```

This is not a strict one-to-one chain. A career may have several pathways. A pathway may lead to several courses. A course may support several careers. The structure exists to help students navigate the concepts without treating them as identical.

## 13. Privacy and security considerations

CareerBridge may be used by minors and young students. Privacy is therefore part of the product design rather than an optional technical detail.

### 13.1 Data minimization

The platform should collect only information that contributes to guidance. It should avoid requiring exact addresses, sensitive financial documents, or unnecessary personal details.

### 13.2 Anonymous use

Students can begin the counselling journey anonymously. An HTTP-only journey cookie allows the experience to be resumed. When a student signs in, the anonymous journey can be attached to the account.

### 13.3 Account data

Student-owned profiles, saved items, comparisons, action plans, and Mentor conversations must be protected so that one student cannot access another student’s data.

### 13.4 Administrator protection

Workbook uploads, row review, approval, rejection, resynchronization, and audit information must be restricted to authorized administrators.

### 13.5 Security work still required

Before broad public release, the project should complete:

- Authorization testing for every user-owned operation.
- Authorization testing for every administrator operation.
- Rate limiting for sign-in and Mentor requests.
- Workbook size and content restrictions.
- Secure secret management.
- Sensitive-log review.
- Database backup and restoration testing.
- Error monitoring and incident response procedures.

## 14. Technology overview

CareerBridge is implemented as a modern web application using:

- Next.js for the web application and server-rendered routes.
- React for interactive user experiences.
- TypeScript for typed application code.
- Tailwind CSS and shared design components for the interface.
- PostgreSQL for user, catalogue, import, and planning data.
- Drizzle ORM for database access and schema management.
- SheetJS for reading Excel workbooks.
- Playwright for browser-level testing.
- Optional AI providers for Mentor responses.
- A retrieval layer for finding relevant approved information.

The application uses server-rendered pages for data-heavy routes and focused client-side components for interactions such as counselling, tabs, saved items, Mentor chat, and import review.

## 16. Known limitations

### 16.1 Data is not automatically current

The platform cannot assume that a source URL or old workbook record is still correct. Current deadlines, fees, eligibility rules, and course availability need periodic verification.

### 16.2 Some catalogue data is incomplete

Institution websites, contact details, hostel information, exact coordinates, and structured course mappings are not complete for every record.

### 16.3 Fallback data can hide configuration problems

Development fallback data is useful when a database is not available. A production deployment must clearly show whether it is connected to the canonical database or operating with fallback content.

### 16.4 Recommendation ordering requires testing

The rule-based recommendation engine is explainable, but every ordering system can create unintended bias. It requires representative student profiles and periodic review.

### 16.5 The Mentor is not a substitute for official confirmation

Even when the Mentor provides a useful answer, students should check official sources for important application facts.

### 16.6 Administration requires operational discipline

The import workflow reduces risk, but it does not remove the need for human review. Administrators must understand warnings, verify important records, and maintain the refresh schedule.

## 17. Recommended operating process

A reliable operating process should include the following cycle:

### Step 1: Collect or receive a source

Obtain a workbook, official page, official notice, or other approved source. Record the source name, URL, date, and intended dataset.

### Step 2: Import into review status

Do not publish new data immediately. Preserve the original source and create review rows.

### Step 3: Normalize

Normalize district names, stage labels, URLs, dates, arrays, stable keys, and verification states while preserving the original value.

### Step 4: Review warnings and errors

An administrator should review missing values, duplicate keys, ambiguous course mappings, unusual geography, and source problems.

### Step 5: Approve or reject

Only approved rows should become canonical student-facing records. Rejected rows should remain auditable.

### Step 6: Publish with freshness information

Display the approved record with source, verification state, and last-checked information where appropriate.

### Step 7: Refresh volatile facts

Recheck dates, fees, deadlines, eligibility, and current availability according to an agreed schedule.

### Step 8: Record the change

Store who approved or changed the record, what changed, when it changed, and which source supported the change.

## 18. Development and maintenance guidance

### 18.1 Before changing the system

A developer should first identify whether the requested change affects:

- Student flow.
- Profile or counselling state.
- Recommendation logic.
- Catalogue data.
- User-owned data.
- Administrator permissions.
- Source verification.
- Database schema.

Changes to one area may affect several others. For example, adding a new counselling answer may require changes to validation, profile snapshots, recommendation factors, persistence, and test coverage.

### 18.2 Before adding new data

Confirm:

- The stable key.
- The intended geographic scope.
- The source URL.
- The verification status.
- The last-checked date.
- Whether the value is stable or volatile.
- Whether the data should be reviewed manually.

### 18.3 Before changing recommendations

Test at least:

- A Class 10 student.
- A Class 12 student.
- A student with strong academic signals.
- A student who is uncertain.
- A student with a low budget.
- A student who prefers to remain near home.
- A student open to vocational or diploma routes.
- A student whose answers contain conflicting signals.

The system should provide useful possibilities in every case without showing unsupported certainty.

### 18.4 Before changing the interface

Check that every important page communicates:

- The current location in the journey.
- The purpose of the page.
- The most important action.
- A safe way to go back.
- The difference between guidance and official information.

## 19. Local setup overview

The application requires a compatible Node.js environment, npm, PostgreSQL, and the project source.

The general setup process is:

```text
Obtain the project source
        ↓
Install locked dependencies
        ↓
Create a PostgreSQL database
        ↓
Configure the database connection
        ↓
Apply the project schema and migrations
        ↓
Load approved seed or catalogue data
        ↓
Start the development server
        ↓
Run validation and browser tests
```

The environment template defines the database connection and optional authentication, AI, and map configuration. Real credentials must remain private and must never be committed to the project repository.

The primary development checks are:

```text
Install dependencies
Run the development server
Run lint checks
Run TypeScript checks
Create a production build
Run browser tests when the database and preview are available
Run catalogue validation when the workbooks are available
```

The exact deployment and migration procedure should be standardized before production use so that developers do not accidentally apply changes to the wrong database.

## 20. Testing strategy

### 20.1 Functional tests

Functional testing should confirm that:

- A student can start counselling.
- Answers are validated and saved.
- A student can resume an incomplete journey.
- A completed profile leads to recommendations.
- Recommendations explain why they appeared.
- Field details connect to careers and routes.
- Two options can be compared.
- An action plan can be created and updated.
- Saved items persist correctly.
- A student can sign in and recover progress.

### 20.2 Data tests

Data testing should confirm:

- Stable keys are unique.
- Required fields are present.
- URLs have valid shape.
- District names are normalized.
- Stage values use controlled vocabulary.
- Geographic scope is correct.
- Verification states are preserved.
- Workbook rows do not bypass review.

### 20.3 UX tests

UX testing should include desktop and mobile layouts. Testers should observe whether a student can answer:

1. Where am I?
2. What is this page showing me?
3. What should I do next?
4. Can I change my mind?
5. Which information is verified and which still requires checking?

### 20.4 Security tests

Security testing should verify:

- Students cannot access another student’s data.
- Ordinary users cannot access administrator operations.
- Anonymous journey identifiers cannot be guessed.
- Uploads are restricted and validated.
- Authentication requests are rate-limited.
- Sensitive information does not appear in logs.

## 22. Conclusion

CareerBridge is a student guidance platform built around a clear need: students should be able to understand their choices before they are expected to make them.

The project already contains the foundation for a real service. It has a guided counselling flow, an editable profile, explainable recommendations, a regional catalogue model, exploration pages, comparison tools, action plans, an optional Mentor, and an import-review foundation for structured data.

The project should now be developed with discipline rather than by adding features without control. The most important priorities are trustworthy regional data, official-source verification, security, maintainable operations, and user testing.

A successful CareerBridge experience should leave a student with three things:

1. A clearer understanding of their possible directions.
2. Enough information to compare those directions responsibly.
3. One realistic next step they can take without feeling pressured or lost.

That is the central purpose of the project.
